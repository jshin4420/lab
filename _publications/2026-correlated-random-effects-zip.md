Kindergarten letter-sound fluency assessments often produce longitudinal count data characterized by excess zeros and substantial between-student heterogeneity. This study applies zero-inflated Poisson mixed-effects models to data from 461 kindergarten students assessed three times during an academic year.

Models with correlated and independent random effects are compared, and a simulation study motivated by the empirical data evaluates the consequences of ignoring random-effect dependence. Results show that accounting for correlation improves model fit and estimation efficiency, whereas assuming independence produces wider confidence intervals and attenuated variance estimates when dependence is present.

**Keywords:** zero-inflated Poisson models; mixed-effects models; correlated random effects; hierarchical count data; two-part models
