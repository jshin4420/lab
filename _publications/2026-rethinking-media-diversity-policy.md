This study examines how media diversity policies are being reconfigured in platformized media environments. Through comparative analysis of the European Union, the United Kingdom, France, and Australia, it uses a four-dimensional framework encompassing structural, content, distribution, and user diversity.

The analysis suggests that media diversity policy is shifting beyond ownership regulation toward broader governance frameworks addressing exposure diversity, platform accountability, social representativeness, and media literacy. Diversity is considered not only as the existence of multiple media providers but also as the conditions enabling citizens to access, encounter, and critically engage with diverse information.

**Keywords:** media diversity; media pluralism; platform governance; exposure diversity; media policy; platformization; comparative media policy
