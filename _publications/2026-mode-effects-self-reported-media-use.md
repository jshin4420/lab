The use of mixed-mode surveys has expanded substantially in media and communication research, raising concerns about the comparability of estimates obtained through different survey modes. This study examines mode-related differences in self-reported OTT media use time using questionnaire, online survey, and diary-based measures from South Korea.

Three harmonization approaches—regression adjustment, coarsened exact matching, and multiple imputation—are compared. Across all approaches, online surveys produced higher OTT use estimates than questionnaire- and diary-based measures. Adjustment substantially reduced the online–questionnaire discrepancy, whereas a moderate online–diary difference persisted. The findings suggest that mode-related differences reflect both respondent composition and reporting dynamics.

**Keywords:** mixed-mode surveys; survey mode effects; harmonization; self-reported media use; measurement equivalence
