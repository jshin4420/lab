## Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of **statistical methodology, computational data analysis, media and communication, and public policy**. I use rigorous quantitative methods and large-scale empirical data to study how audiences, platforms, and media markets interact as media consumption becomes increasingly fragmented.

## Quantitative & Computational Methods

A central part of my research is the development and application of statistical and computational approaches to complex media and social science data. I work with large-scale longitudinal and cross-sectional datasets, including the **Korean Media Panel Survey (KMPS)**, to investigate media use, audience behavior, and platform dynamics.

My methodological work includes **statistical matching, survey data integration, nearest-neighbor matching, regression adjustment, coarsened exact matching, multiple imputation, mixed-mode survey harmonization, mixed-effects models, zero-inflated models, correlated random effects, simulation studies, and computational analysis of large-scale media datasets**.

### Selected Methodological Work

**Statistical Matching & Data Integration**  
Integrating heterogeneous survey datasets when media-use behaviors and psychosocial attributes are measured in separate samples, using nearest-neighbor matching, distance-based matching, simulation, and distributional validation.

**Survey Harmonization & Measurement**  
Evaluating mode effects in self-reported media use using regression adjustment, matching, and multiple imputation to distinguish respondent composition effects from reporting dynamics.

**Longitudinal Count Models & Simulation**  
Assessing correlated random effects in zero-inflated Poisson mixed models through simulation and empirical analysis of longitudinal count data.

### 01 — Audience Fragmentation

How are audiences distributed across television, OTT, YouTube, and emerging platforms? I examine changing media-use patterns and the ways audiences assemble increasingly complex media repertoires.

### 02 — Cross-platform Substitution & Supplementation

When audiences move between platforms, are they replacing one medium with another, or adding new forms of media use? A central goal is to develop better empirical measures of substitution, overlap, and audience movement across platforms.

### 03 — Media Markets, Competition & Advertising

How do changing audience behaviors reshape competition among broadcasters, OTT services, platforms, and advertising markets? This line of research connects audience behavior with market structure, competitive pressure, and regulatory change.

### 04 — Statistical Methodology for Media Research

How can statistical methods capture complex media behavior that conventional surveys and single-platform measures miss? Current work focuses on survey integration, statistical matching, mixed-mode measurement, longitudinal models, simulation, and reproducible quantitative frameworks for media research.

### 05 — Media Diversity & Platform Governance

How should media diversity be understood when distribution and exposure are increasingly mediated by digital platforms? This research examines the transition from traditional ownership-centered approaches toward broader frameworks of exposure diversity, platform accountability, and democratic communication.

## Research Trajectory

My academic training combines **journalism and advertising** with a **Ph.D. in statistics**, followed by research on media, audiences, markets, large-scale survey data, and public policy at KISDI since 2012. This interdisciplinary background motivates a research program that uses statistical and computational methods to address substantive questions in media and communication.
